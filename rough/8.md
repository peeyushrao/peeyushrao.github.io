## What is a true positive with low business risk?

A **true positive with low business risk** is a security event that correctly identifies **real malicious or policy‑violating activity**, but where the **potential impact to the organization is minimal**. The threat is genuine, yet the affected asset, data, or conditions significantly limit business harm.

This commonly occurs when verified malicious activity targets **low‑criticality systems**, such as isolated lab machines, test environments, or non‑production workstations that do not contain sensitive or mission‑critical data.

**In simple terms:**
> The threat is real, but the risk to the business is low.

---

## Why True Positive ≠ High Risk

Not every confirmed attack requires emergency response. Risk is determined not just by **malicious intent**, but by:
- What was targeted
- What data was exposed
- How far the attacker progressed
- Whether controls successfully limited impact

A mature SOC distinguishes between **detection accuracy** and **business impact**.

---

## Key Characteristics of Low‑Risk True Positives

Analysts classify an event as a low‑risk true positive by evaluating environmental and contextual factors.

### Asset Criticality
- The affected system is not essential to business operations
- Loss of the system would not cause:
  - Financial harm
  - Operational outage
  - Reputational damage

### Containment and Isolation
- The asset resides in a segmented or restricted network
- Lateral movement to high‑value systems is prevented

### Data Sensitivity
- No storage or processing of:
  - Personally Identifiable Information (PII)
  - Financial data
  - Intellectual property

### Attacker Sophistication
- Activity originates from:
  - Automated tooling
  - Opportunistic scanning
  - Low‑skill attackers (e.g., script kiddies)

Rather than targeted campaigns or advanced persistent threats (APTs).

---

## Alternative Definition (Operational View)

A true positive with low business risk is malicious or suspicious activity that:
- Is correctly identified
- Is contained early
- Targets low‑value assets
- Fails to progress through the attack lifecycle

Strong preventive and detective controls reduce impact even when detection confirms malicious behavior.

## CrowdStrike Falcon Perspective

In **CrowdStrike Falcon**, low‑risk true positives are common and must be handled **without overreaction**.

### Typical Examples

- Malware execution involving:
  - A non‑privileged user
  - A non‑critical workstation

### Falcon Response Outcome

- Falcon detects execution
- Malicious process is blocked or terminated
- Persistence is prevented
- Lateral movement does not occur

The threat is real, but Falcon’s controls successfully limit impact.

---

## SOC Handling Guidance

Low‑risk true positives should:
- Be documented and tracked
- Inform detection tuning and metrics
- Not trigger disruptive containment actions

They provide **valuable signal** without requiring high‑urgency response.

---

## Key Takeaway

- **True positive** confirms malicious activity
- **Low business risk** reflects limited impact

A mature SOC responds proportionally—acknowledging real threats while focusing effort on events that **materially endanger the business**.
