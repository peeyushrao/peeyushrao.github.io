### Why are accurate timestamps critical during investigations?

Accurate timestamps are the chronological backbone of any cybersecurity
investigation. They allow analysts to determine exactly when an event
happened, reconstruct the sequence of activity, and correlate logs
across disparate systems to build a unified narrative of the intrusion.

The Role of Timestamps in Analysis

> • Reconstruction and Attribution: Timestamps provide answers to
> critical questions regarding who was involved and how an event
> transpired by placing system activity in temporal order.
>
> • Log Correlation: Analysts rely on consistent time data to link
> events from different sources---such as matching a firewall connection
> log to a specific process execution on an endpoint.
>
> • Identifying Anomalies: Timestamps help uncover \"timestomping,\" a
> technique where attackers modify file metadata to make malicious files
> appear legitimate by matching the creation dates of surrounding system
> files.
>
> • Calculating Dwell Time: Accurate timing is essential for measuring
> dwell time (how long an attacker remained undetected) and breakout
> time (how fast they moved laterally)

Beyond simple correlation, timestamps are vital for forensic validation.
For example, in NTFS, if a file\'s \'Modified\' time predates its
\'Created\' time, it often indicates the file was copied to the system
rather than being native to it. Furthermore, detecting timestomping is a
high-fidelity indicator of a human adversary attempting to hide their
tracks by manipulating metadata\
\
Accurate timestamps are critical because incident response is
fundamentally a timeline problem. Without reliable timestamps, you
cannot confidently determine what happened first, what caused what, how
far an attacker progressed, or whether activity is related at all.

Bad timestamps lead to wrong conclusions, missed scope, and incorrect
response decisions.

In CrowdStrike Falcon, investigations rely heavily on time-based
correlation across:

- Host Timeline

- Process execution events

- Detection sequences

- Incident graphs

- Cross-host activity

Accurate timestamps allow you to:

- Reconstruct parent → child execution correctly

- Determine pre- and post-execution behavior

- Identify attacker dwell time

- Correlate activity across hosts and users

- Align endpoint activity with identity, network, and cloud logs

Example:

- A credential access detection after execution = escalation

- The same detection before execution = likely benign/system behavior

Without accurate time, that distinction collapses.
