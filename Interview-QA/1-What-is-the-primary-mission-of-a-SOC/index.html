    1 What is the primary mission of a SOC

    What is the primary mission of a Security Operations Center (SOC)?¶

    The primary mission of a “Security Operations Center (SOC)” is to identify and respond to threats to allow an organisation to continue to operate safely even in the face of cyberattacks. It serves as a centralised “Command and Control (C2)” hub tasked with resisting digital attacks and minimising the impact of any successful compromise. Ensuring that the business can remain operational despite constant adversarial threats. This is achieved by moving beyond simple prevention to a proactive model of “prevention, detection, and response”. Core Functions and Objectives

    The SOC operates through a continuous cycle of activities designed to protect information systems and assets:

    •   Monitoring and Surveillance: Continuous scanning of network traffic, endpoints, and applications to identify unusual or potentially hostile activity.
•   Incident Triage: Evaluating incoming alerts from security tools to differentiate between “true positives” (actual threats) and “false positives” (digital noise).
•   Investigation and Analysis: Conducting a deep dive into forensic artifacts to determine the scope, chronology, and origin of an intrusion.
•   Remediation and Recovery: Taking immediate action to contain a threat, eradicate malicious elements (such as malware), and restore systems to a known-good state.

    Specifically, the SOC is responsible for the entire “incident lifecycle”: starting with the continuous monitoring of logs to find anomalies, triaging those alerts to identify real threats, and leading the technical response to contain and eradicate an attacker from the environment. Ultimately, the mission is to reduce “dwell time” —the duration an attacker remains undetected—thereby limiting potential financial, reputational, or data loss."

    When you define the SOC’s mission, experienced interviewers look for these key concepts:

    •   Operational Continuity: Mentioning that the goal isn't just to "stop hackers" but to ensure the business continues to function.
•   Intelligence-Led Approach: Understanding that the SOC mission is guided by threat intelligence to prioritise the most likely and impactful threats.
•   Continuous Improvement: Recognising that the SOC must learn from every incident to improve future detective and preventive controls.
•   Accountability: Knowing that the SOC provides the "single point of truth" and accountability for security events across the entire organisation.

    CrowdStrike Perspective From a CrowdStrike Falcon perspective, the SOC’s mission is to: a. Detect adversary behavior early using Falcon Insight / XDR telemetry b. Investigate activity in context using: • Process Tree • Host Timeline • Event Search (e.g. ProcessRollup2, ImageLoad, NetworkConnect) c. Respond decisively with: • Host containment • Process termination • Real Time Response (RTR) • Falcon Fusion (SOAR) automation d. Continuously improve detections by tuning IOAs and correlation rules based on real incidents

    In CrowdStrike environments, a SOC succeeds when: Attackers are disrupted before achieving objectives — not when alerts are merely closed.

    MITRE ATT&CK Mapping The SOC mission spans all ATT&CK tactics, but focuses heavily on: TA0001 – Initial Access TA0002 – Execution TA0003 – Persistence TA0008 – Lateral Movement TA0006 – Credential Access TA0040 – Impact A strong SOC aims to detect attackers before Impact.

    MITRE ATT&CK Mapping

    The SOC supports the organisation across the entire MITRE ATT&CK matrix, but its mission focuses heavily on the later stages of the attack lifecycle:

    • Detection: Identifying techniques related to Discovery (TA0007), Lateral Movement (TA0008), and Command and Control (TA0011).
• Response: Mitigating the Impact (TA0040) of adversarial actions, such as data destruction or resource hijacking
