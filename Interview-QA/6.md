### What is alert fatigue and why is it dangerous?

Building on our previous discussions about context and risk-driven
decisions, it becomes clear that without these filters, a SOC would
quickly be overwhelmed by alert fatigue.

Alert fatigue occurs when security analysts are overwhelmed by the sheer
volume of notifications generated by security systems, many of which are
false positives or low-severity issues. This condition arises from the
constant flood of information that must be collected, categorised, and
interpreted every day.

Why Alert Fatigue is Dangerous

The primary danger of alert fatigue is that it degrades an
organisation's ability to defend Itself effectively:

- Missing Critical Threats: When analysts are inundated with noise, they
  can easily overlook high-fidelity alerts that indicate a real, active
  compromise.

- Operational Errors: A \"fried\" or exhausted analyst is far more
  likely to make mistakes during triage, such as incorrectly closing a
  genuine incident or misinterpreting log entries.

- Increased Dwell Time: Because critical signals are buried in the
  noise, attackers can remain in the environment for months without
  being detected.

- Analyst Burnout: The work becomes monotonous and exhausting, leading
  to high staff turnover and a loss of institutional knowledge.

- False Sense of Security: Overreliance on technology that generates
  excessive noise can lead to complacency, where the team assumes that
  because \"nothing important\" was found yesterday, nothing is wrong
  today.

Scenario

Scenario: An EDR tool triggers a \"Medium\" alert for a single instance
of whoami.exe on a financial server.

- The Fatigue Failure: Because the analyst has seen 100 similar
  \"informational\" alerts that week caused by a legitimate IT auditing
  script, they close the alert without looking at the parent process.

- The Reality: The whoami.exe was actually spawned by a malicious
  Microsoft Word macro (a weaponized document).

- The Result: The attacker successfully executes a privilege escalation
  and moves laterally, all because the initial signal was dismissed as
  noise.

In CrowdStrike Falcon, alert fatigue is explicitly addressed by design.

CrowdStrike reduces fatigue by:

- Separating events from detections

- Correlating detections into incidents

- Prioritizing behavior chains, not single events

- Using IOAs + ML, not raw signatures

Where fatigue still appears:

- Overly broad custom IOAs

- Poorly tuned correlation rules

- Treating every detection as equal risk
