## Why is context more important than severity alone?

**Severity is a static label; context explains risk.**

Without context, severity can be misleading. A *low‑severity* alert may represent an active breach, while a *high‑severity* alert may be completely benign. Context provides the **big picture** needed to determine whether a signal represents real adversarial activity or simple digital noise.

Severity is typically a score based on potential impact. **Context**, however, answers the critical investigative questions: **who**, **where**, **how**, **what happened before**, and **what could happen next**. This is what allows analysts to identify intent and prioritize response effectively.

---

## 1. Differentiating Threats from Routine Activity

High‑severity alerts viewed in isolation are often false positives. Context is what separates routine operations from real threats.

### The "Normal" Baseline
- To recognize abnormal behavior, analysts must first understand what *normal* looks like in their environment
- Activities that appear dangerous in one organization may be standard in another

### Process Context
- Processes like `svchost.exe` are common on Windows systems
- Context such as **disk location**, **parent process**, and **execution chain** determines whether it is:
  - A legitimate system service
  - A malicious binary masquerading as a trusted process

Without this context, severity alone cannot reliably indicate risk.

---

## 2. Accurate Prioritization and Triage

Relying solely on severity often leads to **alert fatigue**, where analysts are overwhelmed by high‑severity noise and miss real threats.

### Situational Awareness
- Context helps determine:
  - Attacker dwell time
  - Current stage of the attack lifecycle
  - Whether activity is isolated or part of a broader campaign

### Resource Allocation
- Threat intelligence adds context about:
  - Which vulnerabilities are actively exploited
  - Which industries or environments are targeted

This allows teams to prioritize **medium‑severity risks under active exploitation** over **critical‑severity risks that are theoretical or inactive**.

---

## 3. Enabling an Actionable Response

Context transforms raw notifications into **actionable intelligence** by answering the investigative question: *so what?*

### Correlation
- Correlating timestamps and events across:
  - Endpoints
  - Firewalls
  - Proxies
  - Identity logs

This builds a timeline that reveals the **origin, progression, and scope** of an incident.

### Attribution and Intent
- Understanding adversary **Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs)** provides context to:
  - Predict next steps
  - Identify lateral movement paths
  - Choose effective containment actions

Severity does not explain intent. Context does.

## CrowdStrike Falcon Perspective

In **CrowdStrike Falcon**, severity is only a **starting point**. Investigations are driven by contextual signals such as:

- Process ancestry (parent → child relationships)
- User identity (standard user vs admin vs service account)
- Host criticality (workstation, server, domain controller, executive laptop)
- Behavioral sequence (execution → credential access → lateral movement)
- Environmental baseline (first‑time behavior vs common activity)

## Example: Low Severity, High Risk

### Scenario
- Low‑severity PowerShell detection
- Executed by `winword.exe`
- Encoded command present
- Followed by outbound network traffic

**Result:** High‑risk incident despite low severity

- Parent process: `excel.exe`
- User: Finance executive
- Followed by: Network connection to unknown external IP

**Severity Says:** Low risk  
**Context Says:** Active compromise

---

## Key Takeaway

- **Severity prioritizes alerts**
- **Context prioritizes risk**

A mature SOC does not respond to alerts based on severity scores alone. It responds based on **contextual understanding of attacker behavior**, business impact, and attack progression.
