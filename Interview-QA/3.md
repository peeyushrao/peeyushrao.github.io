**Why is context more important than severity alone?\
\**
Severity is a static label, while context explains risk.\
Without context, severity can be misleading --- a "low-severity" alert
may represent an active breach, while a "high-severity" alert may be
benign.\
Context answers the critical questions: who, where, how, what happened
before, and what could happen next.

Context is more important than severity alone because it provides the
\"big picture\" necessary to determine whether a suspicious signal is a
legitimate threat or merely digital noise. While severity is a score
assigned to an event based on its potential impact, context includes the
specific details---such as the user, device, timing, and relationship to
normal activity---that allow an analyst to identify adversarial intent
and prioritise the response effectively.

1\. Differentiating Threats from Routine Activity

A high-severity alert in isolation can often be a false positive.
Without context, a system might flag a massive data transfer as a
critical exfiltration event, when it is actually a routine, authorised
backup.

> • The \"Normal\" Baseline: To identify what is abnormal, an analyst
> must first understand what \"normal\" looks like in their specific
> environment.
>
> • Process Context: A process like svchost.exe is common on Windows
> systems; however, the context of its location on disk or its parent
> process determines if it is a standard system service or a malicious
> binary hiding in plain sight.

2\. Accurate Prioritisation and Triage

Relying solely on severity can lead to alert fatigue, where critical
threats are missed because the queue is filled with high-severity
\"noise\".

> • Situational Awareness: Context helps analysts determine the dwell
> time (how long an attacker has been in the system) and which stage of
> the attack lifecycle has been reached.
>
> • Resource Allocation: Threat intelligence provides the context needed
> to predict which vulnerabilities are being actively exploited in your
> specific industry, allowing the team to prioritise patches for
> \"medium\" risks that are actually under active attack over
> \"critical\" ones that are not.

3\. Enabling an Actionable Response

Context transforms a raw notification into actionable intelligence by
answering the \"so what?\" of an investigation.

> • Correlation: By correlating timestamps across different logs
> (firewalls, endpoints, proxies), responders can build a chronology of
> the incident to understand its origin and scope.
>
> • Attribution and Intent: Understanding the Tactics, Techniques, and
> Procedures (TTPs) used by an adversary provides the context to know
> how they move laterally, what they are searching for, and how to
> effectively contain them.
>
> In CrowdStrike Falcon, severity is only a starting point. What
> actually matters is context, such as:

- Process ancestry (parent → child chain)

- User identity (standard user vs admin vs service account)

- Host criticality (server, domain controller, exec laptop)

- Sequence of behaviors (execution → credential access → lateral
  movement)

- Environment baseline (first-time behavior vs common)

Example:

- A Low-severity PowerShell detection

- Executed by winword.exe

- Encoded command

- Followed by outbound network traffic\
  ➡️ High-risk incident, despite low severity

**Falcon shows:**

Low-severity alert: cmd.exe /c whoami

Context reveals:

- Parent: excel.exe

- User: Finance executive

- Followed by: Network connection to unknown IP

Severity says: Low

Context says: Active compromise
