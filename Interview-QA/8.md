What is a true positive with low business risk?\
\
A true positive with low business risk is a security alert that
correctly identifies an actual attack or policy violation (a True
Positive), but where the potential damage to the organisation is
negligible because the target is of low criticality. A true positive
with low business risk is activity that is correctly identified as
malicious or suspicious, but has limited impact on the organization due
to factors like early detection, strong controls, low asset criticality,
or failed attacker progression.

This typically occurs when a verified threat targets an isolated system,
a non-production lab environment, or a machine that contains no
sensitive \"crown jewel\" data.

To classify an event in this category, analysts look for specific
environmental factors:

• Asset Criticality: The system involved is not essential for business
operations and its unavailability would not cause financial or
reputational harm.

• Containment/Isolation: The asset is on a segmented network, preventing
the attacker from performing lateral movement to high-value areas.

• Data Sensitivity: The machine does not process or store Personally
Identifiable Information (PII), intellectual property, or financial
records.

• Attacker Sophistication: The threat may originate from a \"hobbyist\"
or script kiddie using automated tools for a nuisance attack rather than
a targeted campaign by an Advanced Persistent Threat (APT).\
\
In other words: The threat is real, but the risk to the business is
minimal. In CrowdStrike Falcon, this situation is very common and must
be handled correctly to avoid overreaction.

- Malware execution on:

  - A non-privileged user

  - A non-critical workstation

- Falcon:

  - Detects execution

  - Blocks or kills the process

  - Prevents persistence or lateral movement
