## What is the primary mission of a Security Operations Center (SOC)?

The primary mission of a **Security Operations Center (SOC)** is to **identify, investigate, and respond to security threats** so that the organization can continue to operate safely—even in the presence of active cyberattacks.

The SOC functions as a centralized **command‑and‑control hub** responsible for resisting digital attacks, minimizing the impact of successful compromises, and ensuring **business continuity**. Modern SOCs move beyond pure prevention to a proactive model of **prevention, detection, and response**.

Ultimately, the SOC’s mission is to reduce **attacker dwell time**—the period an adversary remains undetected—thereby limiting financial, operational, reputational, and data loss.

---

## Core Functions and Objectives

The SOC operates through a continuous, cyclical workflow designed to protect systems and data.

### Monitoring and Surveillance
- Continuous visibility across endpoints, networks, and applications
- Identification of unusual or potentially malicious activity through logs and telemetry

### Incident Triage
- Evaluation of alerts generated by security tools
- Differentiation between **true positives** (real threats) and **false positives** (noise)

### Investigation and Analysis
- Deep forensic analysis to determine:
  - What happened
  - When it happened
  - How it happened
  - The scope and origin of the intrusion

### Remediation and Recovery
- Containment of active threats
- Eradication of malicious artifacts (e.g., malware, persistence mechanisms)
- Restoration of systems to a known‑good state

---

## The Incident Lifecycle Responsibility

The SOC owns the **entire incident lifecycle**, including:

1. Continuous monitoring for anomalies
2. Alert triage and prioritization
3. Technical investigation and root‑cause analysis
4. Containment, eradication, and recovery

The mission is not merely to close alerts, but to **stop adversaries and restore secure operations** as quickly as possible.

---

## What Interviewers Look for in a SOC Mission

Experienced interviewers expect the SOC mission to reflect the following principles:

### Operational Continuity
- The goal is not just to “stop hackers,” but to ensure the business continues functioning

### Intelligence‑Led Approach
- SOC operations are guided by threat intelligence to prioritize the most likely and impactful threats

### Continuous Improvement
- Lessons learned from incidents are used to improve detections, playbooks, and controls

### Accountability
- The SOC serves as the **single source of truth** for security events across the organization

## CrowdStrike Perspective

From a **CrowdStrike Falcon** perspective, the SOC’s mission is to:

### Detect Early
- Identify adversary behavior using Falcon Insight and XDR telemetry

### Investigate in Context
- Process Tree analysis
- Host Timeline reconstruction
- Event Search (e.g., `ProcessRollup2`, `ImageLoad`, `NetworkConnect`)

### Respond Decisively
- Host containment
- Process termination
- Real Time Response (RTR)
- Falcon Fusion (SOAR) automation

### Continuously Improve
- Tune Indicators of Attack (IOAs)
- Improve correlation rules based on real incidents

**In CrowdStrike environments, a SOC succeeds when attackers are disrupted before achieving objectives—not when alerts are merely closed.**

## MITRE ATT&CK Alignment

The SOC mission spans the entire MITRE ATT&CK framework, with heavy emphasis on detecting and stopping attackers before they reach impact.

### High‑Priority ATT&CK Tactics
- **TA0001 – Initial Access**
- **TA0002 – Execution**
- **TA0003 – Persistence**
- **TA0006 – Credential Access**
- **TA0008 – Lateral Movement**
- **TA0040 – Impact**

A strong SOC aims to detect and disrupt adversaries **before Impact occurs**.

---

## Detection and Response Focus

Across the ATT&CK lifecycle, the SOC emphasizes:

### Detection
- Discovery (TA0007)
- Lateral Movement (TA0008)
- Command and Control (TA0011)

### Response
- Mitigation of Impact (TA0040), including data destruction, ransomware, or resource hijacking

---

## Key Takeaway

The SOC exists to **protect business operations under attack**.

- Detection finds the threat
- Response stops the threat
- Improvement ensures it happens faster next time

A mature SOC is measured by **how quickly attackers are identified, contained, and removed—not by alert volume or ticket closure rates**.
